Moving Out of a Lake Nona Apartment: What to Do With What Is Left

Getting a deposit back means an empty unit, and Lake Nona's HOA and apartment rules make the curb a bad plan. Here is the sequence that works.

Every move ends with the same pile: furniture that will not fit the new place, a mattress not worth moving, and a mountain of flattened boxes.

In Lake Nona that pile has nowhere obvious to go, because both the apartment complexes and the HOA communities have real rules about what can sit outside.

On this page

  1. The curb is not available here
  2. Start with what is actually worth something
  3. Donate what is clean and sound
  4. Cardboard is its own problem
  5. Book the last pass before your final walkthrough

The curb is not available here

Most Lake Nona communities restrict what can be left outside and for how long, and enforcement is genuinely active in a way it is not everywhere.

Apartment dumpsters are for household trash. Putting a sofa beside one typically gets the cost of removing it billed to your unit, which comes out of the deposit you are trying to protect.

Start with what is actually worth something

Two weeks before the move, not two days. Anything sellable takes time to sell.

After that window closes, switch from selling to donating. Holding a dresser for another week hoping for a buyer is how people end up with a full apartment on move-out day.

Donate what is clean and sound

Charities can take furniture they can sell: clean, structurally sound, no rips or heavy staining. They cannot take a water-damaged particleboard bookshelf, and asking them to absorb a disposal cost is not a donation.

Several organisations in the Orlando area will collect larger furniture. Book that collection early, because the good ones schedule out.

Cardboard is its own problem

A one-bedroom move generates a startling volume of cardboard. Flattened, it compresses well and it is clean recyclable material.

Check whether your complex has a dedicated cardboard recycling area before you assume it does. Many do, and a stack beside a dumpster that has one is a violation rather than a recycling attempt.

Book the last pass before your final walkthrough

Whatever is left after selling and donating needs to be gone before the walkthrough, not after.

Schedule it for the morning of, or the day before. A hauler takes it from inside the unit, which is the whole point. You do not carry anything down to a curb that will not accept it anyway.

The short version

Sell early, donate what is clean, recycle the cardboard properly, and have anything left taken from inside the unit before your final walkthrough rather than after.
