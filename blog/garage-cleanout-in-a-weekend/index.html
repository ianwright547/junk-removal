Garage Cleanout in a Weekend: A Plan That Actually Finishes

Most garage cleanouts stall because of sorting, not lifting. Here is a two-day plan built around that, including what to pull out before a hauler arrives.

Garage cleanouts fail for a predictable reason. People start by opening boxes, get absorbed in the contents, and four hours later the garage is worse than when they began because everything is now on the driveway.

The fix is to decide the rule before you touch anything, then apply it without reopening the decision.

On this page

  1. Saturday morning: decide the rule first
  2. Saturday: four zones, no fifth
  3. Saturday afternoon: pull the hazardous material out now
  4. Saturday evening: dry out the paint
  5. Sunday morning: photograph the Gone pile and book
  6. Sunday: the donation run
  7. What to ask the hauler

Saturday morning: decide the rule first

Before anything moves, write down what earns a place in this garage. Vehicles, lawn equipment, tools you used in the last two years, holiday decorations, sports gear in active use.

Everything else is leaving unless there is a specific reason it stays. Having the rule written down means you are applying a decision rather than making a hundred new ones.

Saturday: four zones, no fifth

Mark four areas with tape or boxes. Keep. Donate. Hazardous. Gone.

There is no maybe pile. The maybe pile is where garage cleanouts go to die, because it gets put back in the garage at the end of the weekend and you have achieved nothing.

Saturday afternoon: pull the hazardous material out now

Do this before you book anyone, because no hauler can take it and finding out on the day wastes everyone's time. Into the hazardous zone:

  • Liquid paint, stain, solvent and thinner
  • Gasoline, oil and fuel cans
  • Propane tanks including small camping cylinders
  • Pesticides, herbicides and pool chemicals
  • Car batteries
  • Anything unlabelled or leaking

Saturday evening: dry out the paint

Small amounts of leftover paint can be turned into ordinary waste. Stir in cat litter or sawdust, leave the lid off overnight, and by morning it is solid. A solid can is just a can.

Larger quantities are not worth the effort. Take them to household hazardous waste collection with the rest.

Sunday morning: photograph the Gone pile and book

Photos get you an accurate quote. A verbal description of a garage pile is optimistic every single time, from everybody, without exception.

Take four or five shots from different angles, including anything unusually heavy or awkward, and send them with the quote request.

Sunday: the donation run

Do this yourself if you have a vehicle, because it is the part a hauler adds the least value to. Clean, working, sellable items only. A charity is not a disposal service and cannot take a broken exercise bike.

What to ask the hauler

Three things before they load:

  • Is that the final price or an estimate?
  • Is there anything in this pile you cannot take?
  • Do you sweep up?

The short version

The lifting is not the hard part and it is the part you can pay someone to do. Sorting is the hard part, and the only way through it is deciding the rule before you start and refusing to build a maybe pile.
