Shed and Fence Removal After a Storm: What to Do First

A damaged shed or downed fence is a safety and insurance problem before it is a cleanup problem. Here is the order that protects your claim.

Florida storms take out sheds and fences more than almost anything else, and the instinct afterwards is to start clearing immediately.

Slow down for one afternoon. What you do in the first day affects whether your insurance pays for any of it.

On this page

  1. Photograph before you touch anything
  2. Call your insurer before you hire anyone
  3. Make it safe, and stop there
  4. Storm debris versus ordinary debris

Photograph before you touch anything

This is the step people skip and regret. Photograph the damage from multiple angles, wide and close, before anything is moved.

An adjuster cannot assess damage that has already been hauled away. If you clear first and claim second, you are relying on their goodwill and your description.

Call your insurer before you hire anyone

Ask two questions: is this covered, and do you need to inspect before removal?

Some policies cover detached structures and fencing, some do not, and some require an inspection before debris is cleared. Find out which yours is before a truck arrives.

Make it safe, and stop there

There is a difference between making a site safe and clearing it. Safe means no exposed nails or sharp metal where people walk, nothing that will blow into a neighbour's property in the next storm, and no standing water collecting where mosquitoes will breed.

Tarp it, tape it off, secure the loose pieces. Then leave the rest until you have the insurance answer.

Storm debris versus ordinary debris

If your county is running post-storm collection, storm-damaged fencing and shed material usually qualifies as construction and demolition debris, which is collected separately from vegetative debris.

What does not qualify is everything that was inside the shed. Contents are ordinary household junk regardless of the storm, and mixing them into a debris pile can get the whole pile skipped.

The contents are usually the hazardous part

Sheds store exactly what cannot be hauled: fuel cans, pesticides, pool chemicals, old paint, propane cylinders.

After a storm these are often damaged or leaking, which makes them more urgent and more dangerous. Separate them, do not let them sit in standing water, and take them to household hazardous waste collection.

Rebuild permits are a separate question

Removing a damaged accessory structure and building a new one are different from a permitting standpoint, and requirements vary between Orange County, Osceola County and incorporated cities like Belle Isle and St. Cloud.

Check with your local building department before you commit to a rebuild. Nobody hauling debris can answer this for you.

The short version

Photograph it, call your insurer, make it safe, and only then clear it. Reversing that order is how people end up paying out of pocket for damage that was covered.
