Helping a Family Member Whose House Has Become Unmanageable

What actually helps, what makes it worse, and where junk removal fits. Written by people who do the hauling side of these jobs.

We get called by families, usually adult children, usually after something has forced the issue: a fall, a code enforcement letter, a landlord.

We do the hauling. We are not clinicians. But we have been in a lot of these houses, and there are patterns in what works and what backfires badly.

On this page

  1. The surprise clean-out almost always backfires
  2. It is usually not about the objects
  3. Start by asking what they want to change
  4. Go slowly and let them hold the veto
  5. Know the line between junk removal and remediation
  6. Get professional support involved
  7. If there is a deadline you cannot move

The surprise clean-out almost always backfires

The instinct is to arrange everything while the person is away and present a cleared house as a relief.

It reliably produces acute distress, it destroys trust, and the accumulation typically returns faster than before. Whatever the pressure, do not do this to someone.

It is usually not about the objects

Hoarding disorder is a recognised mental health condition and it responds to treatment. It is not laziness and it is not a cleaning problem.

A house cleared without the underlying condition being addressed fills again. That is not a failure of effort, it is what happens when only the symptom is treated.

Start by asking what they want to change

Not what you want changed. Almost everyone in this situation wants something different: to use the kitchen, to have a grandchild visit, to get the landlord off their back.

Working toward a goal they named is the difference between help and an assault on their home.

Go slowly and let them hold the veto

Room by room, session by session, with the person deciding. Nothing leaves without agreement.

It is slower. It is also the only version that tends to hold.

Know the line between junk removal and remediation

This is the practical part, and it determines who you should be calling.

Heavy accumulation of ordinary household material is junk removal: volume, sorting, carrying, disposal. Human or animal waste, decomposition, serious mould or an active infestation is biohazard remediation and needs a licensed contractor. Ask any company directly which one they are. A company that claims both without licensing is one to avoid.

Get professional support involved

A therapist experienced with hoarding disorder, or a professional organiser trained in it, changes outcomes more than any amount of hauling.

The International OCD Foundation maintains resources and directories for hoarding specifically, and it is a better starting point than searching generally.

If there is a deadline you cannot move

Sometimes there genuinely is: an eviction, a condemnation notice, a hospital discharge that needs a safe home.

In that case be honest with the person about the deadline rather than hiding it, and get as much of their agency into the process as the timeline allows. Even choosing the order of rooms helps.

The short version

Never clear the house as a surprise. Work toward a goal the person named, go at their pace, and know whether you need a junk removal crew or a licensed remediation contractor before you call anyone.
