What to Do With the Mountain of Cardboard After a Move

A single move generates a startling volume of cardboard. Here is how to get rid of it without annoying your neighbours.

Nobody plans for the cardboard. You plan for the furniture, the utilities and the cleaning, and then the move ends and there is a wall of flattened boxes in the garage.

Cardboard is at least one of the easier problems, because it is genuinely recyclable and genuinely wanted.

On this page

  1. Flatten everything first
  2. Pass it on before you recycle it
  3. Check your complex before you stack it
  4. Curbside recycling has limits

  6. When it is worth paying someone

Flatten everything first

This is not optional advice, it is the difference between one manageable stack and a garage you cannot walk through.

Flattened cardboard compresses to a fraction of its assembled volume, which matters whether you are recycling it, storing it or paying someone to haul it.

Pass it on before you recycle it

Moving boxes have a second life and people actively want them. Local buy-nothing groups, neighbourhood apps and marketplace listings clear a stack of good boxes within a day, usually free.

This is the best outcome: no disposal cost, no processing energy, and somebody else does not buy new boxes.

Check your complex before you stack it

If you are in an apartment, find out whether there is a dedicated cardboard area before you assume there is.

A stack beside a dumpster in a complex that has a cardboard area is a violation rather than a recycling attempt, and in many complexes the removal cost gets billed to your unit.

Curbside recycling has limits

Residential recycling collection generally takes flattened cardboard, but there are usually limits on volume per collection and on how it is presented.

A full move's worth will not go out in one week. Spread it over several collections or find another route.

What is not recyclable

  • Packing tape, which should be pulled off where practical
  • Styrofoam and packing peanuts, which most curbside programs refuse
  • Bubble wrap and plastic film, which jam sorting equipment
  • Wax-coated or heavily soiled cardboard
  • Boxes that got wet and stayed wet

When it is worth paying someone

If you are clearing a whole move's worth at once, if you have furniture going in the same trip, or if you are in a complex with nowhere to put it.

Cardboard compresses well, which usually works in your favour on a volume-priced quote.

The short version

Flatten it, offer it to someone moving next week, and check your complex's rules before stacking anything beside a dumpster.
