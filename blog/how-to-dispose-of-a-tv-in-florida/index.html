How to Dispose of an Old TV in Florida Without Dumping It

Televisions contain leaded glass and heavy metals, and most curbside services refuse them. Here are the real options.

Old televisions are one of the most commonly dumped items in Florida, and it is not because people are careless. It is because almost nothing will take them.

Regular trash collection refuses them, thrift stores refuse the old ones, and they are too awkward for most people to haul themselves.

Why it matters more than it looks

A CRT television, the heavy tube kind, contains several pounds of leaded glass in the screen. Flat screens contain circuit boards with heavy metals, and older LCD backlights can contain mercury.

None of that belongs in the ground. It is also why e-waste processing costs money, which is the real reason so few services accept televisions.

Option one: retailer take-back

Some electronics retailers run take-back programs, occasionally free and occasionally with a fee depending on the item. Terms change, so check the current policy at the retailer rather than assuming.

Option two: county e-waste collection

Orange and Osceola counties both run electronics collection for residents as part of their household hazardous waste programs. This is often the cheapest legitimate route.

Check your county's current schedule, location and accepted-items list directly before driving out with a television in the car.

Option three: donate it, if it actually works

A working flat screen from the last few years can sometimes be donated. A CRT cannot. Thrift operations stopped accepting tube televisions years ago because nobody buys them.

Be honest with yourself about which one you have.

Option four: a hauler

Worth it for a wall-mounted set, a large or heavy unit, several at once, or an office clearing out its equipment.

Before you hand over a computer

Televisions do not store your data. Computers, laptops and some smart TVs do.

Wipe or physically remove the drive before disposal. A hauler is not a certified data destruction service, and for business machines with sensitive data you want a certified ITAD provider, not a junk truck.

The short version

Check your county's e-waste collection first, since it is usually the cheapest legitimate option. Never put a CRT television at the curb, and always wipe a computer before it leaves your house.
