How to Prepare for a Junk Removal Appointment (and Pay Less)

Fifteen minutes of preparation gets you a faster job, an accurate quote and no unpleasant surprises when the truck arrives.

Most of the friction in this business comes from things that could have been sorted out before the truck left the yard.

None of this is complicated and all of it saves you money.

On this page

  1. Take photos before you call
  2. Pull out anything hazardous
  3. Separate what stays
  4. Tell them about access, honestly
  5. Mention the awkward items specifically
  6. Know what you are agreeing to

Take photos before you call

Four or five shots from different angles. Include the whole pile, anything unusually heavy, and the route the crew will use to get it out.

Photos turn a guess into a quote that holds. A description of a garage is optimistic from everybody, every time.

Pull out anything hazardous

Do this before anyone arrives. No hauler can legally take these, and discovering them at quote time wastes everybody's time:

  • Liquid paint, stain, solvent and thinner
  • Gasoline, oil and fuel cans
  • Propane tanks including small camping cylinders
  • Pesticides, herbicides and pool chemicals
  • Car and marine batteries
  • Ammunition and flares
  • Anything unlabelled or leaking

Separate what stays

Tape, a sheet, a sign, or just show the crew. The single most expensive mistake in this business is something being taken that was meant to stay.

If you will not be there, mark it clearly rather than relying on a verbal description.

Tell them about access, honestly

  • Which floor, and whether there is a working elevator
  • Gate codes, garage codes, lockbox details
  • Narrow gates, low branches, soft ground on a long driveway
  • Where the truck can legally park
  • HOA rules that affect timing or approach

Mention the awkward items specifically

Pianos, pool tables, safes, hot tubs, treadmills, large safes and anything bolted down all change the crew size.

Mentioning them when you book means the right number of people arrive. Not mentioning them means a return visit.

Know what you are agreeing to

Ask three things before anything is loaded: is this the final price or an estimate, is there anything in the pile you cannot take, and do you sweep up.

A straight operator answers all three without hesitating.

The short version

Photos before you call, hazardous material pulled out, and what stays clearly marked. Fifteen minutes of preparation removes almost every reason a junk removal job goes wrong.
