Construction Debris Disposal in Florida: What You Can and Cannot Throw Out

Renovation debris rules, weight limits, asbestos in older homes, and when a dumpster beats a hauler.

Renovation generates more material than almost anyone plans for, and a surprising amount of it cannot go in a regular container.

The two things that catch people out are weight and age: heavy material costs differently, and anything from a pre-1980 house may need testing before it is touched.

Heavy material is priced by weight, not volume

Concrete, brick, tile, dirt and roofing shingles hit a truck or container's weight limit long before they fill its space.

A quarter load of concrete can outweigh a full load of furniture. If a quote for heavy material is given by volume alone, expect it to change.

Asbestos: the one that genuinely matters

If the building predates roughly 1980, asbestos may be present in floor tile and the black adhesive beneath it, pipe insulation, ceiling texture, some siding and some roofing.

Undisturbed, it is generally not a hazard. Cut, sanded or torn out, it is. It needs testing first and a licensed abatement contractor if positive. No legitimate hauler will take suspected asbestos material, and one who offers to is a reason to hire someone else.

Lead paint in older homes

Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. Renovation that disturbs it has federal requirements around containment and the certification of the contractor doing the work.

This is a contractor question rather than a disposal question, but it affects the debris and it is worth knowing before demolition starts.

What goes in ordinary debris

  • Drywall, plaster and insulation
  • Lumber, trim and framing offcuts
  • Flooring: carpet, laminate, hardwood, and tile if quantity is flagged
  • Cabinets, countertops and fixtures
  • Doors, windows and siding
  • Packaging, pallets and general site waste

What does not, ever

  • Liquid paint, stains, solvents, thinners and adhesives
  • Fuel, oil and propane
  • Asbestos-containing material
  • Treated wood in some disposal streams, so flag it
  • Anything leaking or of unknown contents

Dumpster or hauler for a renovation?

If debris accumulates over more than about three days, a container is usually cheaper. If it arrives in bursts, scheduled pickups usually win because you only pay for what leaves.

For contractors running several sites at once, scheduled pickups almost always beat a container per job.

The short version

Flag heavy material so it is quoted by weight, and test before you disturb anything in a pre-1980 building. Those two steps prevent both of the expensive surprises in renovation disposal.
